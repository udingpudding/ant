What the sketches decided

  • 01The straight line is the axis — the string at rest. Everything hangs off it.
  • 02The lobes alternate across it: not a squiggle but a standing wave, drawn at both extremes of its swing.
  • 03The axis is also the baseline. The word stands on the same line the wave swings about — one line doing both jobs.
  • 04The A is never drawn. The wave's opening lens stands in its place — and because the axis runs through the middle of that lens, it lands exactly where an A's bar goes. That is why the first lobe is the only solid one and everything after it is dashed. It is the one doing a letter's job.
  • 05Both N's are arches, not diagonals — the lobe one size down. That is the letter no font ships, and the reason there is now a typeface.
  • 06The name is inside the wave, not beside it. Measured off the photograph, the word starts at the second node and its set width is exactly one full period: word and wave finish together.

A correction worth stating plainly

The first photo came in rotated a quarter turn, so I read the axis as vertical and built A–C as tall lockups with the name running down the line. Righted, the sketch is horizontal. J is what you actually drew. A–C are still finished work and still hold up — they are just a different logo from the one on the paper.

What the numbers came from

Nothing in J was chosen by eye. The wave's upper envelope was traced column by column off the photograph, the axis found from its ink profile, and the lettering measured from its bounding box: axis at y = 229, cap height 31 px, amplitude 85 px, half period 112 px, word 217 px. In cap heights that is 2.74 amplitude, 3.61 half period, and a word 7.00 caps long — one full period, to within half a unit.

A family that was cut and dropped

A set in Archivo — heavier, geometric, matching this page's own type — got as far as four finished directions before it was scrapped. It was too thick for the drawing, and it kept arriving at type sitting beside a wave rather than type made out of one. Everything here is Cormorant Garamond Light, the face your portfolio already runs on. The dropped set is in the first commit if it is ever wanted.

The typeface

One curve, five jobs.
Antinode, 54 glyphs, one pen.

In the sketch both N’s are drawn as arches — the wave’s lobe one size down. No shipping font has that letter, so every cut before this one had to substitute a diagonal N and lose the idea. Drawing the face fixes that, and it settles the A as well: the A here is the same arch with a bar through it, which is exactly what the mark does when the axis crosses the opening lens. The drawing never has to write one.

The shoulder in rust. Left to right: the wave’s lobe at the letters’ scale, then the same superellipse standing in as N, as A, inverted as U, and turned a quarter to make the bowl of D. Nothing in the face is placed by hand — every glyph is a skeleton inflated by one pen, and the pen is a single number.

ANTINODE  —  ss01, the round E

STANDING WAVE · THE POINT THAT SWINGS FURTHEST FROM REST

Live text, not a picture of it — the WOFF2 is embedded in this page. Lowercase types the caps, so the face never drops a character.

Why the letter’s arch is not the wave’s sine. Over the same span the two curves nearly agree, but at the node a sine leaves the axis at 63° and a letter has to stand at 90°. The shoulder is a superellipse, exponent 2.5 — flat enough on top to read as a shoulder, vertical where it lands on a stem. It is the curve the pencil actually drew.

Metrics
1000 upem, cap 700, pen 66 — 0.094 cap, measured off the pencil. Glyph width 560 (0.80 cap), sidebearing 91. The sketch runs 0.77–0.83; 0.80 splits it.
Method
Skeletons, miter-offset to a constant width and refitted as cubics. The band measures 66.000 units at every sample; the worst curve fit across the face is 0.44 units, 0.06% of cap.
Coverage
A–Z, 0–9, & ( ) , . : ; - – — ! ? ’ ” /, space. Lowercase mapped to the caps. 78 codepoints, 54 glyphs.
Features
ss01 swaps the E for the round one from your lower sketch. kern carries 16 pairs — the face barely needs more, because A, N, U and the bowls all stand on vertical sides.
Overlaps
Removed with skia-pathops, so these are clean production cuts and not contours relying on winding order to look right.
Extrapolated
Seven letters come straight off your paper — A N T I O D E. The other nineteen are mine, built to the same rules. B G K Q R S W are the ones to look hardest at.

The search that led here

Thin, wide, and spaced out.
Dune, not a game title.

The register those films share — Dune, Passengers, Stowaway — is quiet and technical: hairline weights, wide forms, generous tracking. Nothing shouts. So every face below sits near the light end of its weight axis, and the wide ones are pushed out on width as well. Monoline geometric caps also do something Cormorant cannot. A high-contrast serif has no single stroke for the wave to match, so its pen is a compromise between hairline and stem. A monoline face has exactly one stroke, and the rule hands the wave that number: the curve and the letters become literally the same pen — and as the type gets lighter, the wave thins with it for free. Each weight below is measured off its own font's O, never chosen.

Anybody Expanded Thin 200 / 150

The widest here and the closest to Dune — stretched almost past comfort, which is exactly where those titles live.

Saira Expanded Light 200 / 125

The same move, less extreme. Neutral enough to carry a whole identity rather than one title card.

Encode Sans Expanded Thin 200 / 125

A shade more mechanical than Saira. The flattened O keeps it from turning decorative.

Outfit Thin 200

Pure geometry — circular O, single-storey forms. Passengers rather than Dune: not wide, just quiet.

Josefin Sans Thin 200

Deco bones under the sci-fi. Small caps for its em, so it sets lighter than it measures.

Exo 2 Thin 150

The most adjustable of the set — weight is a dial rather than a decision.

Jura Light 300

The floor of its axis, and still the closest of any of them to the hand in the sketch.

Megrim static

Compass and ruler, nothing else. The most delicate thing on the page and the most fragile — its E is a constructed oddity you either love or redraw.

Orbitron 400, its floor

Kept only to mark the boundary: its lightest weight is still the heaviest thing here. Square sci-fi cannot reach the register those films sit in.

Cormorant Garamond Light the incumbent

High contrast, so the wave's pen can only ever split the difference between hairline and stem.

Nine faces, and the same thing missing from all of them: the arched N. That is what sent this to a drawn alphabet — the section above. These stay on the board because the search is worth seeing, and because if the drawn face is ever the wrong answer, Saira Expanded Light is where I would go instead.

How it is built

Curve
An actual sine, x = A sin(2πy / λ), not arcs. In A–C the amplitude equals half the lobe; in J it follows the photograph, L / A = 1.32.
One pen
J's wave, axis and letters are all 9.43 at cap 100 — not matched by eye but taken from the typeface: it is W / cap of Antinode itself. Change the face's pen and the wave follows.
The word
Tracking is solved, not tasted. 0.007 em is the value that makes NTINODE measure exactly 2 × L; the build asserts it to within half a unit and fails if a glyph ever changes width.
Fit
Four cubic segments per lobe, each carrying the sine's own tangent. Worst deviation from the true curve is 0.09% of amplitude — a tenth of the stroke, so it cannot be seen.
The A
The opening lobe stands 1.38 caps tall and 2.15 caps wide, so it reads oversized among the letters rather than as one of them. The axis crosses it where an A's bar would sit.
Wordmarks
Converted to outlines. No font file is needed to render them, and nothing shifts on a machine without Cormorant installed.
Stroke
Measured, not chosen — 1.4 to 1.55× the thinnest stroke in Cormorant, found at the narrowest point of its O. The carried wave runs at 0.62× that again, so it reads as a trace under the name instead of competing with it.
Joins
Each lobe is one closed contour. Two strokes meeting at a node leave a notch once the pen gets heavy — butt caps cannot form a point.

Two cuts, not one file scaled

A hairline at 24 px is not a light logo.
It is no logo.

Scaled down, the display stroke goes sub-pixel and the mark greys out into a smudge — the renderer has nothing left to draw. So each icon is redrawn rather than resized: same curve, tighter crop, stroke roughly four times heavier, dashes dropped. The size rows on the plates are the icon cut at true pixel sizes; the large lockups are the display cut. Use the display cut above about 80 px and the icon cut below it.
