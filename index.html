What the sketches decided

  • 01The straight line is the axis — the string at rest. Everything hangs off it.
  • 02The lobes alternate across it: not a squiggle but a standing wave, drawn at both extremes of its swing.
  • 03The axis is also the baseline. The word stands on the same line the wave swings about — one line doing both jobs.
  • 04The A is never drawn. The wave's opening crest stands in its place, and the name runs on from there — which is why that lobe is the only solid one and everything after it is dashed. It is the one doing a letter's job.

A family that was cut and dropped

A set in Archivo — heavier, geometric, matching this page's own type — got as far as four finished directions before it was scrapped. It was too thick for the drawing, and it kept arriving at type sitting beside a wave rather than type made out of one. Everything here is Cormorant Garamond Light, the face your portfolio already runs on. The dropped set is in the first commit if it is ever wanted.

Typefaces

Thin, wide, and spaced out.
Dune, not a game title.

The register those films share — Dune, Passengers, Stowaway — is quiet and technical: hairline weights, wide forms, generous tracking. Nothing shouts. So every face below sits near the light end of its weight axis, and the wide ones are pushed out on width as well. Monoline geometric caps also do something Cormorant cannot. A high-contrast serif has no single stroke for the wave to match, so its pen is a compromise between hairline and stem. A monoline face has exactly one stroke, and the rule hands the wave that number: the curve and the letters become literally the same pen — and as the type gets lighter, the wave thins with it for free. Each weight below is measured off its own font's O, never chosen.

Anybody Expanded Thin 200 / 150

The widest here and the closest to Dune — stretched almost past comfort, which is exactly where those titles live.

Saira Expanded Light 200 / 125

The same move, less extreme. Neutral enough to carry a whole identity rather than one title card.

Encode Sans Expanded Thin 200 / 125

A shade more mechanical than Saira. The flattened O keeps it from turning decorative.

Outfit Thin 200

Pure geometry — circular O, single-storey forms. Passengers rather than Dune: not wide, just quiet.

Josefin Sans Thin 200

Deco bones under the sci-fi. Small caps for its em, so it sets lighter than it measures.

Exo 2 Thin 150

The most adjustable of the set — weight is a dial rather than a decision.

Jura Light 300

The floor of its axis, and still the closest of any of them to the hand in the sketch.

Megrim static

Compass and ruler, nothing else. The most delicate thing on the page and the most fragile — its E is a constructed oddity you either love or redraw.

Orbitron 400, its floor

Kept only to mark the boundary: its lightest weight is still the heaviest thing here. Square sci-fi cannot reach the register those films sit in.

Cormorant Garamond Light the incumbent

High contrast, so the wave's pen can only ever split the difference between hairline and stem.

One thing none of them do: in your sketch the N is an arch — the same form as the lobe, just smaller. That makes the mark a system rather than a wave plus a word, and it is the strongest idea on the paper after the implied A. No font ships that N, so it would have to be drawn. Say the word and I will.

How it is built

Curve
An actual sine, x = A sin(2πy / λ), not arcs. In A–C the amplitude equals half the lobe, so each antinode is as wide as it is tall.
Fit
Four cubic segments per lobe, each carrying the sine's own tangent. Worst deviation from the true curve is 0.09% of amplitude — a tenth of the stroke, so it cannot be seen.
The A
The opening lobe stands 1.38 caps tall and 2.15 caps wide, so it reads oversized among the letters rather than as one of them. The axis crosses it where an A's bar would sit.
Wordmarks
Converted to outlines. No font file is needed to render them, and nothing shifts on a machine without Cormorant installed.
Stroke
Measured, not chosen — 1.4 to 1.55× the thinnest stroke in Cormorant, found at the narrowest point of its O. The carried wave runs at 0.62× that again, so it reads as a trace under the name instead of competing with it.
Joins
Each lobe is one closed contour. Two strokes meeting at a node leave a notch once the pen gets heavy — butt caps cannot form a point.

Two cuts, not one file scaled

A hairline at 24 px is not a light logo.
It is no logo.

Scaled down, the display stroke goes sub-pixel and the mark greys out into a smudge — the renderer has nothing left to draw. So each icon is redrawn rather than resized: same curve, tighter crop, stroke roughly four times heavier, dashes dropped. The size rows on the plates are the icon cut at true pixel sizes; the large lockups are the display cut. Use the display cut above about 80 px and the icon cut below it.
