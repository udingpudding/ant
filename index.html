How it is built

Curve
An actual sine, x = A sin(2πy / λ), not arcs. In A–C the amplitude equals half the lobe, so each antinode is as wide as it is tall.
Fit
Four cubic segments per lobe, each carrying the sine's own tangent. Worst deviation from the true curve is 0.09% of amplitude — a tenth of the stroke, so it cannot be seen.
Lens sizing
In E and F the lobe is sized by the word: pick what fraction of its length the word fills, and the amplitude falls out of the clearance needed where the curve is lowest, at the word's ends. Nothing is eyeballed.
Wordmarks
Converted to outlines. No font file is needed to render them, and nothing shifts on a machine without Cormorant or Archivo installed.
Stroke
Measured, not chosen. The serif cut runs 1.55× the thinnest stroke in Cormorant, found at the narrowest point of its O; the grotesque rule runs 0.45× Archivo's. Mark and type read as one instrument.
Joins
Each lobe is one closed contour. Two strokes meeting at a node leave a notch once the pen gets heavy — butt caps cannot form a point.

Two cuts, not one file scaled

A hairline at 24 px is not a light logo.
It is no logo.

Scaled down, the display stroke goes sub-pixel and the mark greys out into a smudge — the renderer has nothing left to draw. So each icon is redrawn rather than resized: same curve, tighter crop, stroke roughly four times heavier, dashes dropped. The size rows on the plates are the icon cut at true pixel sizes; the large lockups are the display cut. Use the display cut above about 80 px and the icon cut below it.
